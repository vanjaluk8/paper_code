## Key Findings
1. DoRA (Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation) decomposes a weight matrix into direction and magnitude components, applying LoRA only to the direction component.
2. Improves parameter efficiency by learning directional updates separately from magnitude scaling.
3. Achieves consistent gains over standard LoRA across a variety of NLU and NLG tasks.
4. Maintains the same inference efficiency as standard LoRA while having the potential to learn better compositional representations.

## Composition Method
- DoRA modifies the LoRA update by normalizing direction and scaling magnitude separately.
- No direct composition method across tasks -- it improves the base LoRA method itself.
- Could serve as a building block for other compositions.

## Relevance to Thesis
- DoRA as a standalone improvement over LoRA can be used in combination with any multi-adapter compositional method.
- Not directly relevant to composition of multiple adapters, but enhancing individual adapter expressivity.

## Limitations / Gaps
- Doesn't compose adapters across tasks. DoRA improves LoRA's expressivity and efficiency at the single-adapter level.
- No composition mechanism for multiple adapters is proposed.
