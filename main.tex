% Abstract — single paragraph, no blank lines, max ~200 words
\abstract{Multi-task large language model inference on resource-constrained
hardware faces a fundamental tension: full fine-tuning is prohibitively
expensive, yet parameter-efficient serving systems assume centralised
orchestration. This systematic literature review asks whether a peer-to-peer
(P2P) direction is theoretically grounded: can task-specific adapters be
discovered, retrieved, composed, and served across autonomous nodes sharing a
frozen backbone without a central coordinator? Following PRISMA 2020 and
a single-wave Wohlin et al.\ snowballing pass, 1{,}150 snowball candidates were
examined; 162 records passing screening were merged with a 352-paper
pre-validated corpus,
yielding 123 included records (120 distinct papers) across five clusters:
parameter-efficient fine-tuning, adapter composition, LLM inference serving,
mixture-of-experts routing, and P2P/federated learning. Each study was
appraised on a six-dimension rubric, and the evidence was synthesised into a
unified gap map. Across the 17 systems compared in the review's concept
matrix---the most developed published exemplars of each pillar---none
simultaneously addresses a frozen shared backbone, adapter-level exchange, P2P
topology, decentralised discovery, multi-task fusion, and the absence of a
central coordinator; the literature instead partitions into four gap
quadrants---conceptual, algorithmic, systems, and empirical---each defining
open research questions. This establishes the evidence base and research
agenda for decentralised, adapter-based P2P inference.}
